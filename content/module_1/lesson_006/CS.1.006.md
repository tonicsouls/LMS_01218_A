"
[Sound of a heavy vault door closing, then electronic scrambling]

**[IMAGE 1: A surgical laser cutting into a vault door]** We are moving from large-scale net attacks to surgical strikes. This is **Business Email Compromise**, or BEC. 

**[IMAGE 2: A chessboard with a King piece being targeted by a red laser]** The attacker no longer wants your personal password. They want the keys to the corporate treasury. 

**[IMAGE 3: A row of executive faces in a glass-walled boardroom]** In a BEC attack, the intruder impersonates a high-ranking executive—the CEO, the CFO, or a senior Vice President. 

**[IMAGE 4: A folder labeled "CONFIDENTIAL" being opened by a digital hand]** They use research. They know who you report to. They send a simple, plain-text email. No links. No attachments. 

**[IMAGE 5: A text message bubbles appearing in rapid succession: "URGENT", "DO IT NOW"]** Just a high-pressure request. 'I’m in a confidential meeting. I need an urgent wire transfer to this new vendor. Don't call me, just get it done.' 

**[IMAGE 6: A human figure being pushed by a giant thumb labeled "AUTHORITY"]** It leverages **Authority Bias.** You want to be helpful. You want to avoid the 'consequences' of a delay. 

**[IMAGE 7: A timeline where an email leads directly to a large bag of money vanishing]** This is where the human barrier is most vulnerable. The request sounds legitimate because the persona is legitimate. 

**[IMAGE 8: A telephone receiver glowing gold beside a computer screen]** The defense is a physical override: **Out-of-Band Verification.** If a request involves money, you must verify it using a second, trusted channel. 

**[IMAGE 9: Two hands shaking across a table, one digital, one human]** Call the executive on their known office line. Never follow the instructions in the email itself. Two-factor your financial protocols. 
"
